Keep an encrypted digital journal: private, offline and well organised

A journal is personal. With a sealed notebook it stays that way on your computer: encrypted, offline and readable only with your password.

Why a digital journal?

On a computer you write faster, can search entries and put photos or files right beside them. To keep the journal private anyway, it needs protection that fits the subject. Mumbin stores everything locally and offers three kinds of protection, from single pages to a completely sealed notebook.

The best option: a sealed notebook

When you create a new notebook, Mumbin asks whether you want to protect it with a passphrase. If you do, everything is encrypted: every page, every attachment and even the names on disk. Anyone who opens the folder sees only unreadable files. At start you enter your passphrase, and the journal opens.

One entry per day

  1. Create a page Journal with subpages per year or month.
  2. Write one page per day, for example 2026-11-20. With a page template, every entry starts with your usual layout.
  3. Add tags such as @grateful or @ideas to find entries by topic later.
  4. Add photos and other files as attachments.

Finding and looking back

Search finds words and phrases in every entry. The tag cloud shows which topics occupy you most. One thing to know: locked pages deliberately do not appear in hits – as long as the journal is locked, everything stays hidden.

Good habits

  • Choose a passphrase you can remember and use nowhere else.
  • Keep it safe, because there is no back door.
  • Back up the notebook folder regularly on a second drive.

How the encryption works in detail is explained in the article How secure is Mumbin? You find the steps to set it up in the guide “Encrypt notes”.

Frequently asked questions

Are the page names of a journal encrypted too?

Yes, if you seal the notebook when you create it. Then every page, every attachment and even their names on disk are encrypted.

Can I protect only some entries?

Yes. Every page can get a password of its own. You can mix open and protected pages in one notebook.
